# Predictions: Caching Agent

**About Shaping Agent:** They likely traced the full call chain from `GlyphCollection.flush()` through `WorkerBridge.buildBatchBuffers()` to the synchronous `shapeText()` loop that runs on the main thread, confirming that all ~100K `hb_shape()` calls block the UI. They probably identified `buffer.json()` as the hidden cost -- the WASM serialize-to-JSON-string then `JSON.parse` round-trip that doubles the per-call expense. Their main proposal is likely replacing `buffer.json()` with direct WASM HEAP32/HEAPU32 reads via `getGlyphInfosAndPositions()`, and moving shaping into 2 dedicated workers (capped at ~32MB total to avoid the earlier OOM at 31 workers).

**About Transfer Agent:** They probably quantified the `postMessage` structured clone overhead at several hundred megabytes for the 951-file batch, since each of the ~3.8M glyph objects must be deep-copied with all 7 numeric properties. Their primary recommendation is likely packing shaped results into `Float32Array` transferables so the worker boundary becomes a zero-copy pointer swap. They may have also explored `SharedArrayBuffer` as a higher-performance alternative but flagged the COOP/COEP header requirement as a deployment complication. They likely noted that the `text` field is redundantly included in each item's transfer payload despite never being read by the builder.
