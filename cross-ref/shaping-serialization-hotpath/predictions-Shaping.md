# Predictions: Shaping Agent

**About Caching Agent:** They likely identified that Cousine-Regular's monospace nature makes per-character shaping results fully deterministic -- each codepoint always yields the same glyph ID and advance. They probably proposed a codepoint-to-glyph lookup table built during font init that would eliminate nearly all runtime WASM calls, since the ~95 ASCII characters cover the vast majority of source code. They may have also noted that `cl`, `ay`, `dx`, `dy`, and `flags` are either unused or trivially reconstructible, meaning the cache entries can be tiny.

**About Transfer Agent:** They almost certainly focused on the structured clone cost of posting shaped JS objects (arrays of 7-field objects per glyph) via `postMessage` to the buffer workers, estimating it at hundreds of megabytes for 951 files. Their main proposal is likely pre-packing shaped data into `Float32Array` transferables for zero-copy worker transfer. They probably also noticed that `item.text`, `item.id`, and `item.options` are dead weight in the payload -- never read by `buildBatchBuffers` -- and recommended stripping them as a quick win before the full typed-array rewrite.
