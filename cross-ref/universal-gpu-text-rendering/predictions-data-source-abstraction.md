# Predictions: data-source-abstraction

## rendering-backend-portability

They almost certainly identified the tight coupling to Three.js throughout the stack -- `GlyphRenderer` extends nothing but imports `THREE` directly, `CodeGrid extends THREE.Object3D`, `PickingSystem` creates `THREE.WebGLRenderTarget` and `THREE.ShaderMaterial`, and the shaders are GLSL ES 3.00 strings embedded inline. Their GPU contract likely maps the 5 instance attributes (position vec3, size vec2, codepoint float, color vec3, groupId float -- 10 floats/40 bytes per glyph) plus the highlight DataTexture and group transform DataTexture as the portable interface that any backend must support. They probably proposed an abstraction layer -- something like a `RenderBackend` interface with methods like `createInstancedMesh`, `uploadBuffers`, `renderPickingPass`, and `updateTexture` -- that Three.js implements today but could be swapped for raw WebGL2, WebGPU, or a native Metal/Vulkan binding. I expect they flagged the picking system's material-swap approach as particularly entangled with Three.js internals and the hardest piece to abstract.

## universal-text-pipeline

They likely traced the pipeline from `textToGlyphs()` through `buildBuffers()` to GPU upload and identified that the font atlas generation (Canvas 2D `measureText` + `fillText` shelf-packing in `GlyphAtlas`) is the browser-specific bottleneck that blocks portability to non-browser runtimes. Their font problem analysis probably concluded that the atlas contract -- a UV map (grapheme -> {u0,v0,u1,v1}) plus per-glyph metrics (width, height, advance) plus a texture image -- is already runtime-agnostic in shape, but the generation depends on Canvas 2D, which would need to be replaced by FreeType/HarfBuzz bindings or pre-baked atlas files for native targets. For the buffer handoff interface, I expect they proposed formalizing the current `{positions, sizes, uvs, colors, codepoints, groupIds, count}` Float32Array bundle as a versioned, serializable interchange format that workers, native code, or even a compute shader could produce, decoupling buffer construction from buffer consumption entirely.

## device-tier-scaling

They likely defined 3-4 device tiers (high-end desktop, mid-range laptop, mobile/low-end, and possibly a "minimal" WebGL1 fallback) based on `maxInstancesPerMesh`, available GPU memory, and `navigator.hardwareConcurrency`. Their memory budget analysis probably calculated that at 44 bytes per glyph (40 bytes instance data + 4 bytes highlight RGBA8), 10,000 glyphs costs ~430 KB of GPU memory, and a 1500-grid scene with ~50K visible glyphs at any time is ~2.1 MB active but ~66 MB total if all grids stay allocated -- manageable on desktop but tight on mobile. Degradation strategies they proposed likely include: reducing `maxInstancesPerMesh` on low-end devices, more aggressive frustum culling budgets (the existing `budget` parameter on `GridVirtualizer`), atlas downscaling (smaller fontSize = smaller texture), disabling the picking pass entirely on mobile, and falling back to synchronous main-thread buffer builds when workers are unavailable. They may also have proposed a content-unloading strategy for off-screen grids (reclaim GPU buffers, re-build on scroll-back) as a more aggressive companion to the current draw-call-only culling.

## prior-art-lessons

They almost certainly analyzed VS Code's text rendering (Monaco editor uses Canvas 2D rasterization per-line, not GPU instancing -- a fundamentally different approach that trades GPU efficiency for simplicity), Alacritty and Wezterm (Rust GPU terminal emulators that use glyph atlas + instanced rendering, very close to glyph3d-js's approach, likely validating the atlas+instancing architecture), and possibly Xi-editor or Zed (which use GPU text rendering with font rasterization pipelines). They may also have looked at mapbox-gl's glyph rendering for SDF (signed distance field) text, which is the main alternative to the bitmap atlas approach glyph3d-js uses -- SDF would enable resolution-independent scaling at the cost of losing sharp pixel-font aesthetics. Their recommendations likely included: consider SDF atlas as a future option for zoom-independent rendering, adopt a pre-baked atlas format for offline generation (like Alacritty's approach), formalize the buffer format as a spec rather than an implicit convention, and possibly look at WebGPU compute shaders for layout computation (moving `textToGlyphs` from CPU workers to GPU). They probably cautioned against over-abstracting the renderer given that Three.js is a well-maintained dependency, suggesting targeted abstraction at the buffer boundary rather than a full HAL.
